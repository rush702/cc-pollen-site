The daily granule · Bee pollen

Flower pollen gathered by honeybees, blended from many regions, and kept cold from the trap to the jar.

  • A rainbow of colors — each hue is a different plant, not a single local bloom.
  • Proteins, free amino acids, B vitamins, minerals, enzymes, and plant flavonoids.
  • Granules, capsules, and tablets. One-month and three-month supplies.
  • Start with 2–3 granules and build toward about a teaspoon (3 grams).

The rare one · Royal jelly

The nutrient-dense food worker bees make for the queen. She is the only bee that eats it for life.

  • Fed to every larva at first. Only the future queen keeps receiving it.
  • Known for proteins, B-complex vitamins, trace minerals, and the fatty acid 10-HDA.
  • CC Pollen offers it in capsules, and folded into honey.
  • Ancient kitchens in Egypt, Greece, and China kept it. The whole food matters more than any one part.

The hive’s seal · Bee propolis

Sap and resin bees collect from trees, mixed with wax, and used to seal and protect the hive.

  • The Greek name means “before the city” — Aristotle’s word for the shield of the hive.
  • A complex plant material: flavonoids, fatty acids, essential oils, and minerals.
  • 500 mg capsules, 60 count, and a propolis salve.
  • CC Pollen runs a U.S. plant built for propolis, not a side line in a honey house.

  • Nine naturally flavored honeys in 6 oz and 12 oz jars.
