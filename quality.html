From trap to jar

Not all pollen is the same food. Proper handling of raw hive food is expensive. Heat stops mold cheaply. CC Pollen has chosen the cold chain since 1978.

The trap

In the 1970s, Royden Brown patented a pollen collection trap and distributed it to beekeepers nationwide. That network became High Desert.

Cold at once

Granules are refrigerated immediately after they leave the hive. They stay cold in shipment. There is no heat treatment to make them shelf-stable. The cold chain is the point.

A rainbow, on purpose

Local pollen is usually a single color — one dominant plant. High Desert pollen is blended from multiple states and seasons so the jar carries many species. The aim is to cover nutrient gaps that show up when local soils are depleted.

A plant for propolis

The Glendale facility is 33,000 square feet of manufacturing floor. It includes a dedicated propolis room — described as the only U.S. plant built specifically for propolis processing.

Tested lots

Batches are laboratory-tested for purity and potency. Production is in GMP-minded facilities. This is quality assurance, not medical treatment.

How to start

  1. Day 1: 2 granules.
  2. Day 2: 4 granules.
  3. Day 3: 8 granules.
  4. Double each day until you reach about 1 teaspoon (~3 grams).

Eat them straight, or stir into yogurt, oatmeal, fruit, or a smoothie. Keep the jar refrigerated or frozen. The cold chain continues after you buy it.

Who should skip hive products

Hive products can trigger severe reactions in people allergic to bees, pollen, or bee stings. These are foods, not treatments for allergy, weight, aging, or medical conditions. Honey is not for infants under 12 months. If you are pregnant, nursing, on medication, or under a doctor’s care, ask that clinician first.
